Prop 03 · Income tax rates

Making the higher-income tax permanent

Voting Yes would make a temporary income tax increase on Californians with higher incomes permanent instead of allowing it to expire in 2031. Most resulting revenue would go toward public education, with additional funding available for health care and other state programs.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 03Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes would make a temporary income tax increase on Californians with higher incomes permanent instead of allowing it to expire in 2031.

  2. 2Income thresholds

    The tax applies to income above certain thresholds, currently around $371,000 for single filers. The thresholds are adjusted for inflation.

  3. 3How the revenue would work

    The measure would direct most of the resulting revenue toward public education and make additional funding available for health care and other state programs.

  4. 4Supporters

    The California Teachers Association, California State PTA, and Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California support the measure. They say it would provide stable funding for schools and health care.

  5. 5Opponents

    The California Taxpayers Association, Family Business Association of California, and California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce oppose the measure. They argue that making the tax permanent could increase California’s already high tax burden and that the state should improve its spending before extending the tax.

Prop 04 · Campaign financing

Public funding for election campaigns

Voting Yes would remove California’s current ban on using public money to fund political campaigns, allowing state and local governments to create public campaign-financing programs if they choose.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 04Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes would remove California’s current ban on using public money to fund political campaigns, allowing state and local governments to create public campaign-financing programs if they choose.

  2. 2How future programs would work

    The proposition itself would not create a campaign-financing program. Future programs would have to follow rules about who could receive public funds, how the money could be spent, and spending limits.

  3. 3Supporters

    The League of Women Voters of California, California Common Cause, and the California Democratic Party support the measure. They say public financing could help candidates without access to wealthy donors compete in elections.

  4. 4Opponents

    The California Taxpayers Association and California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce oppose the measure. They argue taxpayer money should fund public services rather than political campaigns, and that the measure leaves important details to future governments.

Prop 05 · Recall elections

Changing the recall election process

Voting Yes would remove the question asking who should replace a recalled state official. Voters would decide whether to remove the official, and a resulting vacancy would be filled under existing appointment or special-election rules.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 05Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes would remove the part of the recall ballot that asks voters who should replace the official. Voters would only decide whether to remove the official, and the vacancy would then be filled through existing appointment or special-election rules.

  2. 2If the Governor is recalled

    If the Governor were recalled, the Lieutenant Governor would generally become Governor, with certain circumstances allowing for a later election.

  3. 3Supporters

    The League of Women Voters of California and California Common Cause support the measure. They argue that the current system can allow a replacement candidate to win with a small share of the vote and that separating the recall from the replacement election would make the process clearer.

  4. 4Opponents

    California Republican Party leaders oppose the measure. They argue voters should be able to choose a replacement immediately and that Proposition 5 could give politicians control over who fills a vacant position.

Prop 37 · Homebuyer loans

Loans for prospective homebuyers

The measure would give the California Housing Finance Agency up to $25 billion to loan out to prospective homeowners. The loans would cover up to 17% of the price of a newly constructed home.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 37Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Proposition 37 would give the California Housing Finance Agency up to $25 billion to loan out to prospective homeowners.

  2. 2How the loans would work

    Homebuyers would receive private loans to purchase up to 17% of a newly constructed home’s price and repay the loans in monthly installments. Because the loans are private, taxpayers would not bear losses if a homebuyer defaults.

  3. 3Supporters

    Realtors, candidate Xavier Becerra, the California Democratic Party, and the California Conference for Carpenters support the measure. Supporters argue California’s housing market has serious problems and that the measure could allow more Californians to own homes.

  4. 4Potential concerns

    The concerns described include other homeownership costs such as property taxes, repairs, and insurance; uncertainty about where the new homes would be built and how first-generation buyers would be treated; and whether defaults could lead to more debt. Opponents argue that the measure leaves important details unresolved.

Prop 38 · Immunology research

Funding immunology research

The measure would create an $8.4 billion bond for immunology research, including research on cancer, heart disease, and Alzheimer’s. Half would go to a research institute associated with the University of California, and half to nonprofits and other public universities.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 38Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Proposition 38 concerns immunology, the study of the immune system, and would promote research on cancer, heart disease, and Alzheimer’s. An $8.4 billion bond would be created, with half going to a research institute associated with the University of California and half to nonprofits and other public universities.

  2. 2Drug discounts and state revenue

    If drugs were developed, they would be discounted by 20% for Californians, and 10% of profits would go to the state General Fund.

  3. 3Supporters

    The American Association of Immunologists, the California Democratic Party, City of Hope, and the Michelson Center for Public Policy are named as supporters. Supporters argue the measure could help create new drugs that otherwise might not be funded.

  4. 4Opponents’ concerns

    Potential concerns include the overall cost, whether paying up front would cost taxpayers less than paying off a bond in installments, the possibility of limiting state-funded research mainly to immunology, and uncertainty about whether the research would generate profits.

Prop 39 · Voter identification

Government-issued identification for voting

Voting Yes would require voters to present government-issued identification, require election officials to make their best efforts to verify that voters are U.S. citizens, and create a free state-issued voter identification card available upon request.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 39Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes would require voters to present government-issued identification to vote, require election officials to use their best efforts to verify that voters are U.S. citizens, and create a free state-issued voter identification card available upon request.

  2. 2Implementation and cost

    The measure leaves implementation details undefined, so additional legislation would be needed. Government costs are estimated at tens of millions to hundreds of millions of dollars per year.

  3. 3Supporters

    The California Republican Party, Reform California, Californians for Voter ID, and the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association are named as supporters. They argue the measure would increase trust and security in California’s elections.

  4. 4Opponents

    The California Democratic Party, American Civil Liberties Union, Common Cause, League of Women Voters of California, Californians for Voting Rights, California Teachers Association, and SEIU California are named as opponents. They argue the measure could burden voters with disabilities, people with low incomes, married women who have changed their last name, and people who have recently moved.

Prop 40 · Billionaire tax

A one-time tax on billionaires

Voting Yes would create a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of billionaires, with 90% of funding going to health care and 10% going to K–14 education and food assistance.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 40Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal and revenue

    Voting Yes would create a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of billionaires. The proposed allocation would direct 90% of funding toward health care and 10% toward K–14 education and food assistance.

  2. 2Supporters

    Multiple health care worker unions and the California Democratic Party support the measure. They argue that the tax could help replace federal funding cuts to health care, education, and food assistance.

  3. 3Opponents

    Governor Gavin Newsom, gubernatorial candidate Xavier Becerra, the California Teachers Association, and Google co-founder Sergey Brin are named as opponents. They argue the tax could encourage wealthy residents and businesses to leave California, reducing long-term tax revenue.

Prop 41 · Special-tax audits

Audits of programs funded by special taxes

Voting Yes would require the California State Auditor to review programs funded by special taxes before voters decide on them and every four years afterward, checking effectiveness, costs, and accountability.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 41Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes on Proposition 41 would require the California State Auditor to review programs funded by special taxes before voters decide on them and every four years afterward, checking their effectiveness, costs, and accountability.

  2. 2Supporters

    The California Society of Certified Public Accountants, the California Taxpayers Association, Google co-founder Sergey Brin, and the California Republican Party are named as supporters. They argue the proposition would give voters reliable information about government spending and strengthen the state’s preexisting spending limit.

  3. 3Opponents

    The California Democratic Party and SEIU-United Healthcare Workers are named as opponents. They argue the proposition could add unnecessary audit costs and prevent Proposition 40’s billionaire tax from taking effect, reducing funding for health care, education, and food assistance.

Prop 42 · Personal property taxes

Limits on certain new state taxes

Voting Yes would prohibit California from imposing new taxes on the ownership of most financial assets and other personal property, as well as retroactive taxes based on past activities or residency.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 42Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes on Proposition 42 would prohibit California from imposing new taxes on ownership of most financial assets and other personal property, as well as retroactive taxes based on past activities or residency.

  2. 2Supporters

    The California Taxpayers Association, California Chamber of Commerce, and Google co-founder Sergey Brin are named as supporters. They argue the proposition would protect Californians’ savings and retirement accounts from additional taxes and help prevent wealthy residents from leaving the state.

  3. 3Opponents

    The California Democratic Party, multiple health care worker unions, SEIU-United Healthcare Workers, and Senator Bernie Sanders are named as opponents. They argue the proposition could prevent Proposition 40’s billionaire wealth tax from taking effect and limit the state’s ability to raise revenue for public services.

Prop 43 · Local special taxes

A two-thirds threshold for local special taxes

Voting Yes would require local special taxes, including citizen-initiated taxes, to receive a two-thirds vote rather than a simple majority to pass.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 43Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes on Proposition 43 would require local special taxes that are proposed, including citizen-initiated taxes, to receive a two-thirds majority vote rather than a simple majority to pass.

  2. 2Supporters

    The California Republican Party and the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association are named as supporters. They argue the proposition would close loopholes in the law and prevent unnecessary taxes from being imposed.

  3. 3Opponents

    The California State Association of Counties and the League of Women Voters of California are named as opponents. They argue the proposition could make it harder for local governments to fund services such as housing and firefighting, and make it harder to raise revenue.

Prop 44 · Community health clinics

A 90% clinic program-services requirement

Voting Yes would require certain federally funded nonprofit community health clinics to spend at least 90% of their annual revenue on direct health care services, leaving up to 10% for other spending.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 44Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes would require certain federally funded nonprofit community health clinics to spend at least 90% of their annual revenue on direct health care services, limiting other spending to the remaining 10%.

  2. 2Enforcement and costs

    State enforcement costs are estimated in the low tens of millions of dollars annually and could be covered by penalties charged to clinics.

  3. 3Supporters

    SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West (SEIU-UHW West) and Californians for Responsible Healthcare are named as supporters. They argue the measure would encourage transparency and efficiency, with more resources going directly to patients. Supporters also argue it would prevent clinic CEOs from receiving excessively high salaries.

  4. 4Opponents

    The California Democratic Party, California Republican Party, California Chamber of Commerce, California Hospital Association, Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California, and Reform California are named as opponents. They argue the requirement could cut administrative and facility-improvement budgets, force some clinics to close, and increase pressure on emergency rooms.

Prop 45 · Environmental review

Faster environmental review for essential projects

Voting Yes would speed environmental reviews for essential projects, including housing, water, clean energy, and other projects, by creating deadlines for those reviews.

VIDEO EXPLAINERProp 45Open on YouTube ↗
  1. 1The proposal

    Voting Yes on Proposition 45 would allow faster environmental reviews on essential projects including housing, water, clean energy, and others, by creating deadlines for those reviews.

  2. 2Supporters

    The California Hospital Association is named as a supporter. Supporters argue the proposition would increase efficiency by reducing delays and lawsuits while maintaining CEQA’s core protections.

  3. 3Opponents

    The California Democratic Party is named as an opponent. Opponents argue the proposition is unnecessary because of new legislative amendments to CEQA, and that shortening review time could reduce review quality, leading to poor planning and negative impacts for taxpayers.
